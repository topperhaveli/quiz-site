Important date boundary

The uploaded compilation is labelled AY 2026–27 / Finance Act 2025. That is appropriate for income earned in FY 2025–26 under the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026 and governs tax year 2026–27 onwards. Do not use the old-Act section numbers and Finance Act 2025 tables as a complete guide to tax year 2026–27 without transition/current-law updates. Official references: AY 2026–27 tax rates; Income-tax Act 2025 commencement.

Needs correction / clarification

Q23 — Company residency explanation

The answer option about an HUF is correct, but the explanation groups companies with HUFs/firms/AOPs under a control-and-management test. A company’s residence is tested under section 6(3): an Indian company is resident; a foreign company may be resident if its place of effective management (POEM) is in India. Keep the separate tests distinct.

Needs wording precision

Q29 — Income received in India

Clarify “first received in India.” A later remittance to India of income already received abroad is not, merely by being remitted, a second receipt in India. The explanation already gestures toward first receipt, but the stem is broader than that rule.

Needs qualification

Q72 — Employer NPS contribution

The 10% old-regime / 14% new-regime shorthand needs a category qualifier. The deduction limit depends on the employer/category; Central Government employer contributions can be subject to the 14% limit under the applicable rules. Do not memorize this as a universal 10% old-regime rule.

Answer is incorrect / calculation must be redone

Q431 — Agricultural income partial integration (AY 2025–26)

The explanation says the new-regime basic exemption for AY 2025–26 is ₹4 lakh. That is wrong: the new-regime nil slab for AY 2025–26 was ₹3 lakh; ₹4 lakh applied for AY 2026–27. The explanation is wrong, but the key “NIL” may still be correct: partial integration is calculated first, then section 87A rebate is applied if eligible. The source calculation before rebate is ₹8,000 (tax on ₹8.8 lakh ₹38,000 less tax on ₹8 lakh ₹30,000); because the resident individual’s total income is ₹3.8 lakh and AY 2025–26 new-regime rebate can eliminate tax after partial integration, the answer should remain provisional NIL pending a clean worked solution. Do not replace the key solely because the explanation states the wrong nil-slab threshold.
